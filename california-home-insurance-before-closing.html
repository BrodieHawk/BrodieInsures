Homeowners insurance

How to shop for California homeowners insurance before closing

Brodie Williams Insurance Agent with Placer West Insurance Services
Where to start

Ask your lender when proof of insurance is due. Give your agent that deadline, the property address, and the estimated closing date.

Once you have a property address and an estimated closing date, you can start gathering information for a homeowners quote. Starting early gives you time to ask questions about what the policy covers and coordinate paperwork with your lender.

Gather the property details

Have the address, purchase or closing date, year built, and approximate square footage handy. If you know the roof age or when the plumbing, electrical, or heating systems were updated, include that too. A listing or inspection report can help you find those details.

Ask your lender what they need

If you’re financing the purchase, ask your lender what coverage they require, when they need proof of insurance, and how they want the lender listed on the policy. Pass those details to your agent along with any closing date changes.

Compare quotes on the same terms

Look at the dwelling limit, deductibles, exclusions, and any separate policies or endorsements included in the quote. Ask what the proposed policy would cover if the home were damaged by fire, and check how much you’d have to pay out of pocket before coverage applies.

Confirm the policy before closing

Once you choose coverage, confirm that the policy is in place, check its effective date, and ask who will send proof of insurance to your lender.

For more on preparing for closing, see the CFPB closing guide.

When should I start shopping for homeowners insurance?

Start once you have the property address and an estimated closing date. If your inspection raises questions about the roof or other systems, share those early. Ask your lender when they need proof of coverage and give your agent that deadline.

What should I compare on a homeowners quote?

Ask how the quoted dwelling limit was estimated. The purchase price includes the land, while the dwelling limit is about rebuilding the structure. Compare deductibles and ask how the policy would handle personal belongings, liability, and temporary living costs after a covered loss.

What if the first insurer won’t cover the address?

Give your agent any details the first carrier requested and ask what else can be quoted for the property. If you’re offered separate policies, review them together and check with your lender that they meet its requirements.
