Auto insurance

Why is California auto insurance so expensive?

Brodie Williams Insurance Agent with Placer West Insurance Services
Where to start

Get your current and renewal declarations pages. Compare mileage, drivers, discounts, and the full term premium before you shop.

Same car, same commute, no new tickets, and your renewal still went up. Your insurer may have changed its base rates, or details on your policy may have changed. The California Department of Insurance explains that your premium starts with the insurer’s approved rates and then reflects details specific to your policy.

Find out what changed

Compare your renewal with your current declarations page. Check the drivers, vehicles, estimated mileage, where the cars are kept, and discounts. Ask your insurer which items affected the bill. If you drive less than you used to, ask how to update your mileage.

Shop the same coverage first

Compare matching limits, deductibles, and optional coverages. Check the total for the full policy term and any installment fees. A cheaper monthly payment can come with less protection or more out-of-pocket cost after a claim.

Have your declarations page and renewal date handy when you request a quote. You can also use the California Department of Insurance premium comparison guide to see sample rates.

Why did my car insurance go up without an accident?

A clean driving record is still worth checking on your renewal, but it’s only one input. The carrier may have updated its rates or changed how your car is priced. Also check whether a multi-policy discount ended, a household driver was added, or the carrier has an outdated mileage estimate. An agent can help you compare the renewal against an equivalent quote from another carrier.

Does the car I drive affect my insurance cost?

Yes. Before buying or leasing another vehicle, ask for a quote using the specific model or VIN. Compare the premium with the coverage your lender or leasing company requires. Two cars with similar sticker prices can have different premiums.

How can I lower my California auto insurance premium?

Start by correcting mileage and driver details, then ask about discounts you may qualify for. Compare an auto-only quote with a home and auto bundle using the combined policy cost. If you’re considering a higher deductible, compare the savings with the amount you’d have to pay after a covered loss. Review the California Department of Insurance’s auto insurance guide for an explanation of the coverage types.
