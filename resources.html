Insurance questions and checklists

Know what to ask, what to compare, and what to bring to a quote or renewal conversation.

Home and property insurance

How much dwelling coverage should I review?

Start with the estimated cost of rebuilding the structure at today’s labor and material prices. The purchase price and market value reflect factors such as the land and location. A rebuild estimate focuses on the home itself, including square footage, construction type, finishes, and features such as an attached garage.

Tell me about any additions or major renovations so we can review the estimate together. Ask whether the quote includes extra protection if rebuilding costs exceed the dwelling limit and how it handles upgrades required by local building codes.

What options can I explore in a wildfire-prone area?

Availability depends on the property and address. I can help you explore options beyond the California FAIR Plan. To compare quotes, look at how each policy handles fire and smoke damage, which deductible would apply, and whether it includes protection for belongings or temporary housing after a covered loss.

Have your address, recent roof or building updates, and any details about defensible space or other wildfire mitigation work handy. If you have a renewal or closing deadline, share it early so we can account for it while reviewing options.

What is the California FAIR Plan?

It’s a property insurance option for eligible applicants who have trouble finding coverage through the traditional market. If it’s one of the options for your address, we can review which causes of loss are covered, what limits apply, and whether you would need another policy to address gaps such as personal liability.

When comparing a FAIR Plan and companion policy with another quote, look at the total premium and read both policies. Check which policy would respond to a specific loss, what deductible would apply, and whether the coverage dates line up.

What is a DIC policy?

Difference in Conditions (DIC) insurance is one way to add protection alongside a FAIR Plan policy. Depending on the form, it may address gaps involving theft, certain types of water damage, or personal liability.

Read the two policies together. For example, if water damages your kitchen, check whether the cause of the damage is covered, which policy would respond, and which limits or exclusions apply. The exact combination varies by policy.

What should I do if I receive a nonrenewal notice?

Find the effective date and the reason listed in the notice. Keep a copy of the notice and your current declarations page. If the reason involves the condition of your property, gather any records or photos of repairs or improvements you’ve already made.

Reach out early so there’s time to review your current limits, compare available options, and confirm when any replacement policy would take effect.

How does a deductible affect what I pay after a loss?

For a covered property claim with a flat deductible, that amount generally comes out of the covered loss payment. For example, if the insurer determines that $8,000 is payable before a $1,000 deductible, the resulting payment would be $7,000.

Some policies use different deductibles for different causes of loss. If a deductible is listed as a percentage, ask what dollar amount it represents and whether you could comfortably cover it after a loss.

What should I check in my lease before buying renters insurance?

Look for any required liability limit, the date coverage needs to begin, and instructions for providing proof to your landlord or property manager. If you share the rental, check who the policy covers so each roommate can make arrangements for their own belongings.

Then estimate what it would cost to replace your furniture, clothing, and electronics. Ask how the policy values those items and what help is available for temporary living expenses if a covered loss makes the rental unlivable.

Questions to ask about other coverage

  • Renters. Does your lease specify a liability limit or require proof of insurance? How much would it cost to replace your belongings? Renters insurance guide
  • Auto. Who drives each vehicle and how is it used? If you’re considering a home and auto bundle, compare the combined price, limits, and deductibles. Auto insurance guide
  • Umbrella. What liability limits do you already carry, and what limits would the umbrella require on those policies? Umbrella insurance guide
  • Business. What equipment and inventory do you need to insure? Does your lease or a client contract specify any coverage requirements? Business insurance guide

Before you request a quote or renew

  • Find your current declarations page so you can compare limits and deductibles.
  • Note your renewal, move-in, or closing date and bring any lease or lender requirements.
  • Write down what’s changed, such as a renovation, a new driver, or a new business location.
  • Compare quotes with the same limits and deductibles where possible, and ask about exclusions or endorsements that could affect your decision.
