Homeowners insurance

Can I get California homeowners insurance without the FAIR Plan?

Brodie Williams Insurance Agent with Placer West Insurance Services
Where to start

Start with your address and the date you need coverage. If you received a nonrenewal notice, bring it along with any roof and home update records.

One carrier says it can’t cover your address. Does that mean you have to use the California FAIR Plan? It depends on the property and what other insurers are willing to write. If you have a nonrenewal notice or a closing deadline, start with the date you need coverage in place.

Ask an agent to shop the property address

Have the address, current policy or nonrenewal notice, year built, roof age, and any recent updates handy. Mention whether the home is a rental, second home, or vacant. Ask which options are available for your address and what information might help an insurer review it.

Compare what each policy covers

If you find a homeowners option, compare the covered losses, limits, deductibles, and exclusions. If the FAIR Plan is the available option, ask what its basic property coverage includes and whether a separate Difference in Conditions (DIC) policy could address gaps. Review both policies together and check your lender’s requirements.

Confirm coverage before changing policies

Confirm when the replacement policy takes effect and who will send proof to your lender. Wait until you have confirmation that the replacement coverage is in place before canceling existing coverage.

See the California Department of Insurance’s tips for finding residential insurance and its DIC policy resources.

How do I find California homeowners insurance after a nonrenewal?

Check the notice for the date coverage ends and the stated reason. Share both with your agent when you ask for quotes. Have roof replacement records, inspection reports, or information about wildfire mitigation handy if they apply. Ask carriers what documentation they consider for your particular property.

Can I switch off the California FAIR Plan?

If you already have FAIR Plan coverage, you can ask an agent to check whether other options are available. Compare the proposed homeowners policy with your full current setup, including any companion policy. Look at the total annual cost and whether both options cover the losses that matter to you. Confirm that the new policy is in place before changing either existing policy.

What is the difference between a homeowners policy and FAIR Plan coverage with a DIC policy?

A homeowners policy generally bundles several types of protection into one contract. The FAIR Plan provides basic property coverage, and a separate DIC policy can provide additional protection depending on its terms. Ask for a side-by-side review of dwelling limits, liability, water damage, and temporary living expenses. Check which policy would respond and which deductibles would apply.
